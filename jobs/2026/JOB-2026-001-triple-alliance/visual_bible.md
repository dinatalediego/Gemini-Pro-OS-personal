## World Bible

### Battle-stage world

A stylized performance space that can blend 19th-century South American military/political atmosphere with a rap-battle staging language, without modern objects becoming historical claims.

### Historical-context world

Maps, cities, uniforms, documents and battle environments must be treated as evidence-bearing visuals. Any historically specific detail used as factual illustration should be supported by the research corpus.

### Lighting

- strong subject separation for performer scenes;
- consistent key-light logic per performer;
- context scenes may be more documentary and restrained;
- avoid wildly different grading between consecutive shots unless narratively motivated.

## Cinematic Bible

- 16:9 master;
- medium close-ups for verses;
- low-angle hero shots used sparingly;
- controlled push-ins for emphasis;
- wider tableaux for geography/battle context;
- avoid excessive camera motion when dense historical narration is playing;
- keep faces readable enough to preserve consistency.

## Negative Bible

Never introduce without explicit approval:

- modern weapons or contemporary military gear in historical scenes;
- fantasy armor;
- unexplained flags/symbols;
- identity drift between shots;
- wardrobe changes between contiguous shots;
- text inside images that carries factual claims;
- Peru visually presented as a combatant in the alliance war;
- aesthetic changes that make one country a caricature while others remain realistic.
